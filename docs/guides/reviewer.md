# The Legal reviewer's guide

Your desk shows everything waiting on Legal judgement, **oldest first**. That
order is deliberate and not a preference you should want changed: the desk exists
to make the longest wait visible, and a newest-first list buries exactly what it
was built to surface.

## Adjudicating a ticket

A ticket carries **proposed text** — somebody's words, badged so you always know
whose. `VENDOR LANGUAGE` came from the counterparty. `AI CANDIDATE` was drafted
by the model. `EDITED BY LEGAL` has already been through a person's hands.

That text is **quarantined**. Nothing can select it, and it cannot reach a
contract from where it is sitting.

### The approval box is empty on purpose

It is not a bug and it is not an oversight.

If the proposed wording were already in the box, approving would become
confirming — and the rate at which proposed wording is approved *unchanged* is a
figure Legal watches precisely because that pressure is real. A screen that
pre-filled the box would be creating the defect its own measurement exists to
catch.

Copying the proposal in is one keystroke if that is genuinely what you approve.
The screen tells you whether what you have typed matches the proposal or differs
from it, and records which — you do not tick a box for that; it is derived from
the two texts.

### Verify shows you what will exist forever

Pressing **verify** does not mint anything. It shows you the wording, on the
paper surface, as it will be recorded — and then asks.

Read it. A clause version cannot be edited once it exists. There is no undo, and
the origin stamped on it is derived from where the text came from rather than
chosen by anybody.

### Rejecting

A rejection **requires a note**, and the note goes back to the requester. That is
the whole value of rejecting rather than ignoring: the person who asked learns
what would need to change.

Rejection needs no confirmation step, because it mints nothing and a new ticket
can always be opened. Friction belongs where the irreversibility is.

## Override decisions

An override request reaches you only after it has been **socialised** to its
watchers, and you cannot decide it until its **review window has closed**. The
window's purpose is that nobody discovers the override at signature; deciding
inside it would make the socialisation decoration. The screen shows you the
window state before you try, so you never learn this from an error.

**You decide each finding on its own.** There is no approve-all, and its absence
is deliberate rather than missing: accepting a governing-law conflict does not
accept an uncapped indemnity, and one button for both would mean nobody looked at
the second.

Approving a finding opens the gate **for that finding**. Rejecting one requires a
note, like any rejection.

The requester's justification is shown in full, in quotation marks, with any
commercial pressure they cited named separately — those are different claims and
one should not stand in for the other.

## Holds

You can **open** a legal hold. While it is open, nothing about that agreement is
destroyed, however far past its retention date it is.

You cannot **release** one — that is Legal admin's act. You will not find a
greyed-out release button, because a greyed-out button would say "you could, but
not now" and the truth is that this is somebody else's decision.

## What is not yours

The library, the ladders, the conflict rules, owner decisions, retention
destruction. A Legal admin has all of those and everything you have. If you find
a screen offering you one of them, that is a bug worth reporting.
