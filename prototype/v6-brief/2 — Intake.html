Opening a deal — the interview, and where it goes.

The intake interview is the first place a requester meets the model, so it is the screen where the labelling rules matter most. The model asks; the requester answers in their own words; and everything the model then proposes is drawn as waiting until a named person confirms it.

You are being interviewed by a machine

The questions below are written by a language model. Your answers are your own and are recorded as yours. The model will propose a structured summary of what you say; nothing it proposes takes effect until you confirm it, and Legal sees the difference between what you wrote and what the machine made of it.

What confirming does
Each field stamps from waiting to yours, against your name and the minute. The model's proposal stays in the record as what was proposed — confirming does not erase it, and Legal can see where you agreed with the machine and where you did not.
Where the model misread you

What this screen will not do
It will not pre-fill your answer. A screen that wrote your words for you would be interviewing itself.
It will not hide the label. The disclosure at the top is part of the reading order and cannot be dismissed — not an icon, not a tooltip, not a footer.
It will not draw a proposal as a fact. Waiting and confirmed look different at a glance, and they stay different with the colour removed.
There are no sparkles, no gradient badge, no wand. Here the model is a labelled participant with a recorded name, exactly like the people.
